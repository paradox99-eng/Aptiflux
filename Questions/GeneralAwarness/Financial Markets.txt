Financial Markets



Q1. Which is the oldest stock exchange in India and Asia?
A) National Stock Exchange (NSE)
B) Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE)
C) Calcutta Stock Exchange (CSE)
D) Delhi Stock Exchange (DSE)
Answer: B) Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE)

Q2. Who is the primary regulatory authority of the capital market in India?
A) Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
B) Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI)
C) Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
D) Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA)
Answer: C) Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)

Q3. What does 'IPO' stand for in the stock market?
A) Initial Public Offering
B) Internal Public Order
C) Indian Public Offering
D) Investment Portfolio Organization
Answer: A) Initial Public Offering

Q4. The benchmark index of the National Stock Exchange (NSE) is known as:
A) Sensex
B) Nifty 50
C) Bankex
D) Dow Jones
Answer: B) Nifty 50

Q5. A 'Bear' in the stock market refers to:
A) An investor who expects prices to go up
B) An investor who expects prices to fall
C) A broker who charges high commission
D) A person who invests only in government bonds
Answer: B) An investor who expects prices to fall

Q6. Which of the following instruments is traded in the Money Market (short-term market)?
A) Equity Shares
B) Corporate Bonds
C) Treasury Bills
D) Mutual Funds
Answer: C) Treasury Bills

Q7. What is a 'Blue Chip' company?
A) A newly launched startup with high growth potential
B) A highly reputed, financially stable, and well-established company
C) A company that manufactures semiconductor chips
D) A company listed exclusively in the small-cap index
Answer: B) A highly reputed, financially stable, and well-established company

Q8. Mutual funds in India are primarily regulated by:
A) Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
B) Association of Mutual Funds in India (AMFI)
C) Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
D) Ministry of Finance
Answer: C) Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)

Q9. A debt instrument issued by a corporation to raise money from the public is known as a:
A) Treasury Bond
B) Sovereign Bond
C) Corporate Bond
D) Municipal Bond
Answer: C) Corporate Bond

Q10. 'Sensex' is the benchmark index of which stock exchange?
A) National Stock Exchange (NSE)
B) Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE)
C) New York Stock Exchange (NYSE)
D) NASDAQ
Answer: B) Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE)

Q11. The process of converting physical share certificates into an electronic form is known as:
A) Capitalization
B) Dematerialization (Demat)
C) Amortization
D) Liquidation
Answer: B) Dematerialization (Demat)

Q12. What is the main difference between shares and bonds?
A) Shares represent debt, while bonds represent ownership
B) Shares represent ownership, while bonds represent debt
C) Both represent ownership but bonds have voting rights
D) Both represent debt but shares pay fixed interest
Answer: B) Shares represent ownership, while bonds represent debt

Q13. Which of the following is considered a derivative instrument?
A) Futures and Options
B) Treasury Bills
C) Commercial Papers
D) Equity Shares
Answer: A) Futures and Options

Q14. The return earned on a bond is commonly referred to as its:
A) Dividend
B) Capital Gain
C) Yield
D) Premium
Answer: C) Yield

Q15. An open-ended mutual fund is one that:
A) Has a fixed maturity date
B) Can issue and redeem units at any time
C) Trades exclusively on the stock exchange like a stock
D) Invests only in government securities
Answer: B) Can issue and redeem units at any time

Q16. 'NAV' in the context of mutual funds stands for:
A) Net Annual Value
B) Net Asset Value
C) Nominal Asset Value
D) New Asset Valuation
Answer: B) Net Asset Value

Q17. A market condition characterized by a continuous and prolonged rise in stock prices is called a:
A) Bear Market
B) Bull Market
C) Stagnant Market
D) Volatile Market
Answer: B) Bull Market

Q18. Commercial Paper (CP) is an unsecured money market instrument issued in the form of a:
A) Promissory Note
B) Bill of Exchange
C) Cheque
D) Demand Draft
Answer: A) Promissory Note

Q19. Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) wishing to invest in the Indian stock market must register with:
A) Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
B) Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
C) Ministry of External Affairs
D) Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE)
Answer: B) Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)

Q20. Which of the following is a prominent credit rating agency in India?
A) CRISIL
B) AMFI
C) NABARD
D) SIDBI
Answer: A) CRISIL
