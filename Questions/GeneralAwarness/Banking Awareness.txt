Banking Awareness



Q1. When was the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) established?
A) 1st April 1935
B) 15th August 1947
C) 1st January 1949
D) 26th January 1950
Answer: A) 1st April 1935

Q2. Which of the following is NOT a function of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI)?
A) Issuer of currency notes
B) Banker to the government
C) Accepting deposits from the general public
D) Controller of credit and monetary policy
Answer: C) Accepting deposits from the general public

Q3. What does the acronym 'NEFT' stand for in banking?
A) National Electronic Funds Transfer
B) National Economy Funds Transfer
C) New Electronic Funds Transfer
D) Net Electronic Funds Transaction
Answer: A) National Electronic Funds Transfer

Q4. In banking terminology, what does 'NPA' mean?
A) Non-Performing Asset
B) Net Performing Asset
C) National Performing Asset
D) Non-Promissory Asset
Answer: A) Non-Performing Asset

Q5. Which rate is used by the RBI to absorb liquidity from the commercial banks?
A) Repo Rate
B) Reverse Repo Rate
C) Bank Rate
D) Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)
Answer: B) Reverse Repo Rate

Q6. The headquarters of the Reserve Bank of India is located in:
A) New Delhi
B) Kolkata
C) Mumbai
D) Chennai
Answer: C) Mumbai

Q7. What is the maximum insurance cover provided by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) per depositor per bank?
A) Rs. 1 Lakh
B) Rs. 3 Lakhs
C) Rs. 5 Lakhs
D) Rs. 10 Lakhs
Answer: C) Rs. 5 Lakhs

Q8. Which of the following is the largest public sector bank in India?
A) Bank of Baroda
B) Punjab National Bank
C) State Bank of India
D) Canara Bank
Answer: C) State Bank of India

Q9. RTGS is a system where there is continuous and real-time settlement of fund transfers. What does 'RTGS' stand for?
A) Real Time Gross Settlement
B) Real Time General Settlement
C) Rapid Transfer Gross Settlement
D) Regular Time Gross Settlement
Answer: A) Real Time Gross Settlement

Q10. 'KYC' (Know Your Customer) guidelines are issued by the RBI under the provisions of which act?
A) Banking Regulation Act, 1949
B) Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
C) Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002
D) Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881
Answer: A) Banking Regulation Act, 1949

Q11. What does the term 'Bancassurance' refer to?
A) Selling of banking products by insurance companies
B) Selling of insurance products by banks
C) Insurance of bank deposits by the RBI
D) A special type of bank account for insurance companies
Answer: B) Selling of insurance products by banks

Q12. Which of the following is commonly referred to as 'Plastic Money'?
A) Bearer Cheques
B) Demand Drafts
C) Credit Cards
D) Currency Notes
Answer: C) Credit Cards

Q13. What does 'SLR' stand for in the context of Indian banking and monetary policy?
A) Statutory Liquidity Ratio
B) State Liquidity Ratio
C) Standard Liquid Reserve
D) Statutory Lending Rate
Answer: A) Statutory Liquidity Ratio

Q14. The MICR (Magnetic Ink Character Recognition) code present on a bank cheque consists of how many digits?
A) 9
B) 10
C) 11
D) 12
Answer: A) 9

Q15. An IFSC code uniquely identifies a bank branch participating in the NEFT system. It consists of how many characters?
A) 9
B) 10
C) 11
D) 12
Answer: C) 11

Q16. In the Indian banking system, the Base Rate or MCLR (Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate) is decided by:
A) The Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
B) The Government of India
C) The Individual Banks
D) The Indian Banks' Association (IBA)
Answer: C) The Individual Banks

Q17. What is the meaning of a 'Stale Cheque' in banking?
A) A cheque issued without a date
B) A cheque which has completed three months from its date of issue
C) A post-dated cheque
D) A torn or mutilated cheque
Answer: B) A cheque which has completed three months from its date of issue

Q18. Which committee recommended the establishment of Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) in India?
A) Narasimham Committee
B) Kelkar Committee
C) Rangarajan Committee
D) Raghuram Rajan Committee
Answer: A) Narasimham Committee

Q19. In the money market, what is 'Call Money'?
A) Money borrowed or lent for a day
B) Money borrowed or lent for 2 to 14 days
C) Money borrowed or lent for more than 14 days
D) Money borrowed by the government from the RBI
Answer: A) Money borrowed or lent for a day

Q20. NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development) was primarily established to provide credit for:
A) Industrial Development
B) Agriculture and Rural Development
C) Foreign Trade and Exports
D) Urban Infrastructure
Answer: B) Agriculture and Rural Development
